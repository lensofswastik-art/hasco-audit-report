01

The buying decision has moved, and it moved to the website

This is the single fact everything else in this report depends on. It is worth two minutes.

A production manager who needs to raise yield on a flooring line does not start with a phone call any more. They search, they read, they compare, they download a spec sheet, they show it to their plant engineer, and they build a shortlist. Only then do they contact a vendor. By the time Hasko's sales team hears from them, most of the decision is already made, and it was made on pages Hasko controls.

In plain terms

Hasko's sales team is excellent at the 40%. The website is the 60%, and right now it is the part of the process nobody owns.

The website outranks the trade show as a credibility signal

The same research asked technical buyers what makes them trust a supplier. A well-maintained, informative website scored 3.6 out of 5. A prominent trade-show sponsorship scored 2.4. Buyers are close to twice as likely to read the website as proof that a company is serious.

Worth acting on this week

Hasko / Mekanika is a listed exhibitor at IWF Atlanta 2026, 25–28 August, Georgia World Congress Center. haskomachines.com does not mention it anywhere.

Mereen-Johnson runs a site-wide bar carrying its booth number. Eagle Machines lists booth and dates on the homepage. Show week is when search interest in this category peaks, and it is the one week when a buyer who just walked past the Hasko stand will go look the company up.

02

How we audited, and why you can trust the numbers

Nothing here is an opinion about taste. Every figure was measured on the live site.

What we did

  • Walked the live site as a buyer would, on desktop at 1440×900 and on mobile at 390×844
  • Covered the homepage, products index, machine pages, Automation & Integration, Parts & Service, Contact, Careers, News, Testimonials, site search and error handling
  • Computed colour contrast ratios, image alt-text coverage, ARIA attributes and page weight inside the browser rather than estimating them
  • Rendered and assessed three competitors against the same framework
  • Cross-checked every industry statistic against its primary source

What we did not do

  • We did not judge whether the design is attractive. Aesthetic verdicts are not auditable.
  • We did not guess at Hasko's traffic, conversion or revenue. Section 06 sets out the three inputs we need from you before the impact model becomes a business case.
  • We did not reach System TM or Paul Maschinenfabrik in this pass. Both are named competitors, so the benchmark is marked partial.
  • We did not assume the site needs replacing. Several findings are single-token or single-template fixes.

Also worth saying

Hasko's site is fast. 35 requests, around 199KB, content painted in about 1.75 seconds on a representative interior page. Mobile reflows cleanly with a working menu. The specifications, once you find them, are exactly what a mill buyer needs. Whatever gets built next has to hold that line, not trade it away for a heavy page builder.

04

Six findings that matter, and 18 more behind them

We logged 24 findings. These six carry the business consequences. Each opens with what a buyer experiences, then what it costs, with the technical detail one click away.

1

The first screen sells nothing

What a buyer sees today

A rotating photograph of a machine with the model name burned into the image. No sentence saying what Hasko builds or who for. No button. The four industry paths (Flooring, Ripped Products, Dimensional Wood, Moulding) sit below the fold where most visitors never scroll.

What it should do

Say what Hasko makes in one line, offer the four industry paths immediately, and carry a named customer result. Ninety-six years, machines still running after 25, and "Heavy-Built Performance" are all on the site already, as small grey text under the logo.

What it costs. A first-time visitor arriving from a search result or a trade publication has no way to establish, in the first screen, whether Hasko makes what they need. The strongest brand material Hasko owns is set at the lowest visual weight on the page.

Technical detail
  • The homepage <h1> is the literal string "Home". Search engines and screen readers are told the page is about nothing.
  • At 1440×900 the entire first viewport is the carousel. The industry tiles begin below 900px.
  • Mobile at 390×844 follows the same order: menu, carousel, tiles.
  • The carousel auto-rotates with no visible pause control.
  • Model names are rasterised into the slide JPEGs, so they are invisible to search and unreadable by assistive technology.
  • UX law: Jakob's Law. Every comparable manufacturer site opens with a proposition and a primary path; buyers arrive expecting that pattern.
2

The catalogue makes the buyer do the sorting

What a buyer sees today

All 21 machines on one page, in a grid that staggers unevenly. A sidebar of 12 categories that navigates away instead of filtering. Every card's button says the same word, "View". Most names lead with an internal code: HSBU, FXP-EF, PF MPEM-C LP.

What it should do

Two tiers, the way every competitor does it: categories, then machines. Filters by application, material width and horsepower. Cards that carry a one-line description and one capability figure, so a buyer can rule machines in or out without opening them.

This fix is cheaper than it looks

Every product card already carries category data in the markup: category-flooring, category-side-matching, category-planing-surfacing and nine more. The information needed to power filtering is sitting in the page. This is an interface build against data Hasko already has, not a migration.

Technical detail
  • 21 grid items on /products/, each rendered as a WordPress post (post-199 post type-post) rather than a product record.
  • Because they are posts, there is no structured specification data, no Product schema, and no way to filter, compare or syndicate. The only JSON-LD on the page is a generic WebPage graph.
  • The "View" label is a CSS pseudo-element (::after { content:"View" }), not real text, so it cannot be reliably announced, translated or styled as a component.
  • Sidebar categories: Gallery, Material Handling, Feeding Systems, Scanning & Optimizing Systems, Chopping, Board Ripping, Side Matching, Short Stock Feeding, End Matching, Planing/Surfacing, Special Machines, Used Machinery.
  • Product imagery mixes finished photography on white, blue CAD renders, and raw multi-colour CAD assembly exports.
  • UX law: Hick's Law. Decision time rises with the number of undifferentiated options, and none of the 21 can be eliminated without opening it.
3

At the moment of peak intent there is no door

What a buyer sees today

They have read the arbor horsepower, the material width and the feed drive. They are ready. The page offers a phone number in the header and a generic "Contact Form" link in the sidebar that goes to the same four-field form as every other page on the site.

What it should do

A "Request a quote" button in the first viewport of every machine page, pre-filled with that machine, routed to the right person. Mereen-Johnson's says "Contact Us To Learn More". WEINIG's says "Get Pricing".

Technical detail
  • No enquiry element of any kind exists in the product page template. Verified on /sr-series-ripsaw/.
  • The form is Contact Form 7 with fields your-name, your-email, your-subject, your-message. No company, phone, application, machine or timeline.
  • Field labels are placeholder text only, so the label disappears as soon as the buyer types. WCAG 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions and 1.3.1 Info and Relationships.
  • A reCAPTCHA v2 checkbox adds friction and its own accessibility cost.
  • Specifications on the machine page are hidden behind a five-tab control that opens on "Application", which contains one sentence. The Specifications tab holds material width, arbor HP, saw diameter, sleeve diameter, feed rolls and feed drive.
  • The tab controls declare role="tab" and aria-controls but aria-selected is null on all five, so assistive technology is told there are tabs and never told which is open. WCAG 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value.
  • Product pages carry no breadcrumb and no related machines. Machines are bought as lines; the page has no way to move a buyer to the adjacent one.
  • UX law: Fitts's Law. The target for the highest-value action does not exist on the page where intent peaks.

The best proof Hasko owns is invisible

After installing our new Hasko MPEM-C, through put increased by 2k per shift. Yield increased by .5%, added ability to cut 9″ boards (with a much better cut). Out of square boards are a non-issue. Roger Isaacs, SFL Production Manager — haskomachines.com/why-hasko/testimonials/

A named production manager, with a title, reporting a throughput number and a yield number. That page sits inside a whole section (Testimonials, Flooring, Ripped Products, Dimensional Wood, Moulding) that has no entry in the site navigation at all. The only way in is four unlabelled tiles below the homepage carousel.

It appears nowhere on the homepage, nowhere on the products index, and on no machine page. The only proof a buyer meets on a product page is one anonymous pull-quote.

And this is the opening nobody in the category has taken

None of the three competitors we assessed puts a named customer outcome on its homepage. Mereen-Johnson leads with heritage. WEINIG leads with product. Eagle leads with a stock photograph of a mountain range.

86% of engineers look for third-party validation while researching. Putting Roger Isaacs's numbers on the homepage is not catching up with this field. It is doing something none of the leaders currently does.

Technical detail
  • The /why-hasko/ silo contains five built pages and appears in no navigation menu.
  • Several quotes on the testimonials page carry no attribution, which weakens them. Anonymous praise reads as marketing copy.
  • The application-led route is the natural one for most buyers ("I run a truck-flooring line") and it is the route that is hidden. The site currently serves only the buyer who already knows they want an end matcher.
5

The highest-margin business on the site is a phone number in a paragraph

Hasko's own Parts & Service page says the company maintains a database of every machine model, serial number, parts list, drawing, upgrade and customer configuration for the total life cycle of the machine. That database is an asset. The website exposes none of it.

What an owner gets today

No parts lookup. No serial-number search. No parts request form. No manuals, parts lists or exploded diagrams. No ordering. The parts department number sits as unlinked plain text in the middle of a paragraph, under a row of unlabelled photographs of machined components.

What it should be

Machine model to parts list to manual, driven by the records Hasko already keeps. A serial-number field so an owner identifies their exact build. A request form that captures machine, serial, part and urgency. Ordering last, once the first three are being used.

In plain terms

A maintenance manager with a line down at 2am does not call Tennessee. They search, they fail to find, and they buy the part from somebody else, on a machine Hasko built. Every one of those is a high-margin sale leaving, and a relationship weakening before the next machine purchase.

Technical detail
  • The parts number appears as plain text, not a tel: link, inside a body paragraph.
  • The site contains zero mailto: links on any page. Every route is a phone call or one generic form.
  • The Contact page presents nine cards at identical visual weight, eight phone and fax lines plus the address, with no routing guidance and no email addresses. Naming Steve Pugh, Robert Hall and Joey Walker with direct lines is a real differentiator; it is undercut by giving the VP of Sales the same prominence as the fax number.
  • Directions to the plant are two bulleted lists of driving instructions with no embedded map.

05

Where Hasko stands against the field

We rendered Mereen-Johnson, WEINIG USA and Eagle Machines and scored all four sites on the same seven experience dimensions. Eight conventions recur across the field.

The gaps the whole field shares

An audit that only lists what the leaders do well is half an audit. These are the weaknesses that recur across every site we assessed, and they are where Hasko's opening actually is.

Nobody puts named customer results on the front page

Heritage, product and stock photography. Not one named, quantified outcome from a customer. Hasko already owns that material.

Specifications are documents, never data

Every site delivers specs as a PDF or prose. No site in the category lets a buyer ask "which of your machines handles my material width at my throughput" without opening four documents.

Content decay is the category norm

WEINIG's homepage carries a news module reading "No news available" beside a headline dated 2020. Eagle repeats the same stock banner on every page. The site that visibly maintains itself reads as the company that maintains its machines.

Aftermarket is treated as an afterthought

Only WEINIG sells parts online. In a category where a machine runs 25 years and parts carry the higher margin, three of four sites route the aftermarket through a phone call.

06

What this is worth

We will not put a revenue number on a slide we cannot defend. Here is the honest version: the mechanism, the sourced benchmark, and the three inputs we need from Hasko to turn it into a business case.

Three numbers we need from you

S — monthly sessions to haskomachines.com  ·  E — enquiries per month attributable to the site  ·  V & P — average machine order value, and average annual parts spend per installed machine.
With those, every range below becomes a specific figure. Without them, anyone quoting you a revenue lift is guessing.

What to instrument before anything ships

The model above is only as good as its baseline, and Hasko has no product-level measurement today. Before the first wave begins we would put tracking on:

  • Sessions by source to the homepage, the products index and each machine page
  • Machine page to enquiry rate, currently unmeasurable because there is no machine-level enquiry
  • Enquiry volume, and separately qualified enquiry volume
  • Time from enquiry submitted to first meaningful sales response
  • Inbound parts calls per month, and how many are "which part do I need for machine X"
  • Site search queries that return nothing, the cheapest product-gap research available

With those in place every wave becomes falsifiable. That is the point.
